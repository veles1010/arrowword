# Japanese clue review

## Coverage and editorial pass

900/900 canonical English IDs in identical order: 300 Easy, 300 Medium, 300 Hard.
All 900 clues were independently authored from the frozen English sense, then reviewed for sense/part of speech, natural wording, track difficulty, ambiguity, script, concision and phonetic leakage. No translation-through-another-locale or network translation service was used. Turkish was only a secondary sense reference. All six earlier packs remain untouched.

Easy uses direct contemporary definitions. Medium adds a descriptive step; Hard preserves precise distinctions without cryptic wordplay or regional trivia. Japanese/Chinese spacing was not manufactured to mimic English word counts.

## Sense and near-synonym decisions

- COLUMN is a recurring newspaper opinion feature; PILLAR is weight-bearing support.
- CHOICE is the act of selection; OPTION is an available alternative.
- CITE supplies the source as evidence; QUOTE reproduces exact wording.
- HINDER makes task progress harder; IMPEDE obstructs physical movement.
- AMBLE describes slow easy steps; SAUNTER adds leisurely pleasure.
- ENGINE burns fuel for pistons; MOTOR converts electricity into rotation.
- CLOCK is wall-mounted; WATCH is wrist-worn. LIGHT is illumination, not low weight.
- NEST/STEM stay verbs (nested arrangement / arise from a source), never bird home/plant stalk.
- LATENT is present but unmanifest; DORMANT is inactive with possible awakening.
- CREST/PEAK/APEX/ZENITH retain wave/graph/cone/achievement senses; SUMMIT remains a mountain point.
- RIFT describes estrangement; GULF disagreement; CHASM a physical opening.
- FABLE uses animals; PARABLE human events. REVOKE/RESCIND/REPEAL/ANNUL keep permission/decision/law/transaction objects.

Close synonyms still need crossings, as in the approved English source. No new exact duplicate definitions were introduced. Borderline retained usage is limited to the reviewed list above; no English answer spelling or known own-answer phonetic form is supplied.

## Validation limits and device follow-up

The deterministic alias lexicon screens common loan pronunciations; it is not a universal transliteration engine. Script checks screen common Traditional-only forms rather than proving every possible orthographic distinction. Full editorial review complements these checks. Widget tests establish Unicode text survival/layout, not real system-font glyph shape. Review native IME/font rendering on Android/iOS before publication; no font bundle or custom keyboard was introduced.
